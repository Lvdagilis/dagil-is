Why

Most AI reviews stop halfway

A compliance review reads the policies, the risk register and the model card, and never sends a request to the running system. A penetration test sends thousands, and never reads the documents that say what the system is for and who is accountable for it. Compliance frameworks tell you what to document. Threat frameworks tell you what breaks.

AI systems fail in the gap between the two: a human-oversight step that is documented and never exercised, a retrieved document that turns into an instruction, an agent tool whose description quietly redefines what it does. This engagement covers both halves against the same system and produces one set of findings.

What you get

Evidence, not assertions

  • A defensible EU AI Act role and risk classification Provider or deployer, which risk class, and the reasoning written down. Get this wrong and every downstream conclusion is wrong.
  • Findings you can show, not findings you were told Each one quotes what substantiates it: a reproducible payload, a measured rate, a document reference. A finding survives the vendor disputing it.
  • A gap register in the vocabulary your regulator, customer or board uses Every gap mapped to the specific framework reference, prioritised by impact.
  • A remediation roadmap with owners, dates and a re-test Handed over as a tracker, so a fix is closed by verification rather than by an email saying it is fixed.
  • The evidence pack a due-diligence questionnaire asks for Including a methodology and limitations statement that says plainly what was tested, what was not, and why.

What is performed

Four phases, one system

01
Scoping and classification

Your role under the EU AI Act, the risk class, the system boundary and the trust boundaries inside it. An agreed evidence list and written rules of engagement before anything is sent.

02
Governance and documentation review

Policies, risk register, technical documentation, data flows and transfers, human-oversight design. Read, then sampled: a register where nothing ever closes is not in use.

03
Technical and adversarial testing

Red teaming with real payloads: instructions planted in documents and files, data extraction, tool misuse, memory poisoned in one session and recalled in the next. Infrastructure checks no prompt reaches: egress, tenant isolation, rate limits, tool allowlists, confirmation gates. The tool servers an agent trusts, and the models and artefacts in its supply chain. Where the system decides about people, outcome analysis across groups.

04
Reporting and re-test

Findings with severity, owner and date. A statement of what was tested and what was not. A re-test once the fixes land.

Phases 1 and 2 are document work. Phase 3 is where most findings come from.

The whole system

Eight areas, every engagement

Whether the system is an assistant on a website or an agent holding credentials, the same eight areas are examined. Most findings sit at the trust boundaries: every point where data or instructions cross from one level of trust to another.

  • Governance and accountability Someone with the authority to stop it
  • Data governance and transfers Where prompts and payloads physically go
  • Model lifecycle Versioning, acceptance criteria, re-validation on change
  • Trust-boundary enforcement Instruction and data kept apart, at every crossing
  • Access control and secrets Who can reach the model, the data and the weights
  • Supply chain Third-party models, datasets, tools and the servers behind them
  • Monitoring and incident response Drift, abuse, forensic logging, a kill switch
  • Transparency and human oversight Disclosure, explanation, genuine override

Every finding cites the reference it maps to, so the same register answers a regulator, a customer's questionnaire and your own security team. These are the vocabularies the findings are expressed in, not a claim of certification against them.

How it is run

Plainly, and in writing

  • Nothing is sent before it is authorised. Written rules of engagement name the systems, the testing window and the signatory. The tooling refuses anything outside them.
  • Your assessment stays yours. The audit tool runs entirely in the browser, with no backend and no account. It is public at auditai.dagil.is, and your security team can host it inside your own perimeter.
  • Findings go to you and nowhere else. Everything seen or produced is covered by the engagement's confidentiality terms.
  • AI assistance is disclosed. Drafting uses AI assistance. Every finding and every conclusion is reviewed and approved by me before delivery.
  • One person, answering plainly. I will tell you when I am not the right person for the job.
